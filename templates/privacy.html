What Brightboard knows about you

Last updated 8 November 2026. This page describes only what this app actually does — every line below was written against the code that is running.

Who is responsible

Brightboard is built and run by one person. Brightboard is the data controller: it decides what is collected and why. Privacy requests go to {{ support_email }}.

What we collect

WhatWhy we need itThe lawful basis
Your name, your form or grade, your email address To make an account, greet you, and reach you if something breaks Your consent, and performing the service you asked for
Your password — but only as a scrypt hash To let you sign in. We never store or see the password you typed Performing the service
Your date of birth To work out whether you are under 18 and parental consent is needed A legal obligation
Your curriculum and study-style choices To show you the right syllabus and the right kind of practice Legitimate interest
Answers to the starter quiz, and to every practice question you attempt This is the product: it is what the tutor diagnoses from Your consent, and performing the service
Topic scores, per-concept mastery estimates, and the patterns of mistakes we see To pick the one concept worth teaching you next Legitimate interest in providing the service
A paper you upload: its filename, how many pages it has, and the tutor's written analysis of it To teach from your real work. The file itself is not kept — it is read, shrunk inside your own browser, analysed, and then discarded Your consent
The whiteboard lesson script generated for you So you do not wait for it to be written again Legitimate interest

We do not collect your phone's contacts, photos, location, camera roll or browsing history. There is no advertising identifier and no third-party analytics script: Chart.js, jsPDF and pdf.js are served from Brightboard's own /static folder, so your browser asks no one else when you use the app. The only cookie is the signed-in session cookie, which is strictly necessary and is the reason no consent banner appears on this site.

Who we share it with

These are the real processors, and they are all of them:

  • Vercel (hosting) — runs the web app.
  • Neon (Postgres database) — stores your profile, scores and lessons.
  • Google Gemini — writes lessons, marks your answers, reads uploaded papers, and sometimes produces the spoken narration. Your questions and answers are sent to it.
  • OpenRouter — a fallback that relays text to another large-language-model provider when Gemini is unavailable. Same kind of content leaves, different company.
  • Microsoft Edge text-to-speech — receives the words to be spoken aloud when the local narrator is not being used.

We do not sell your data, and we do not rent it. We will disclose it only if the law requires it, which has never happened and is not expected.

How long we keep it, and how it is protected

Until you ask us not to. Nothing expires on its own; deleting your account removes the rows immediately. Traffic is encrypted with TLS, and the database is encrypted at rest by the host. Passwords are hashed with scrypt and a unique salt per account, and are never written to a log. Sign-in attempts are rate-limited and an account locks briefly after repeated failures. Every page of work is read back only to the account that created it — the check happens on the server, not in the page.

Being signed in is not permanent either: the session ends by itself a week after you last signed in, and signing out ends it sooner. Changing your password and deleting your account both ask for the current password first, so a leftover session on a shared phone cannot do either to you.

Your rights

Under the Kenya Data Protection Act 2019 you may access your data, have it corrected, have it deleted, and object to us processing it. You also have the right to lodge a complaint with the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner.

  • Access and take it with you: Account → Download my data. You get every row we hold, as JSON, in your browser.
  • Correct it: Account → your name, form or grade, date of birth, study style and confidence level are editable there, and your password. The curriculum switch sits in the top bar of every page.
  • Delete it: Account → Delete my account and data. One step, no email needed, no persuasion.
  • Object, or ask anything: email {{ support_email }}.

Children

The Kenya Data Protection Act 2019 treats anyone under 18 as a child, and processing a child's personal data requires a parent's or guardian's consent. Brightboard is used by KCSE candidates who are often 15 to 17, so this matters here. We ask for a date of birth at sign-up and use it to spot accounts that need consent.
